# Trophy Hunting Economics

**One-line:** *What guided hunts and trophy tags are worth — pricing, willingness to pay, and the value drivers (species, unit quality, land access) behind the [Luna Hunting Economy](/notes/luna-hunting-economy).*

## What we know (July 2026 research)

Guided-hunt price points (Gila / NM; verify current year):

- **Public-land guided elk** ~$6,500–$9,500 (5-day 1×1); **private-land / landowner-tag elk** ~$8,500–$14,500+ (voucher tag bundled).
- **Mountain lion (hounds)** ~$3,000–$5,500; **black bear** ~$3,000–$5,500; **bear+lion combo** ~$8,500 / 7 days.
- Comparable lodge (Lodge at Chama) published rates: free-range bull elk $14,000–15,000/hunter; cow elk $3,200; spring bear $7,500; mountain lion $6,500; bison $4,500–11,500 (source: Luna Landing Retreat — Master Reference).
- **The bundle is where the margin is:** a raw [E-PLUS](/notes/e-plus) landowner tag nets $5,000–$12,500; bundled into a guided hunt (lodging, meals, guiding, processing) it sells for $8,000–$15,000+ (source: E-PLUS Land Strategy — Luna).
- Typically included: lodging, meals, guide, field care, often airport pickup. Excluded: tag/license, ~8.69% NM sales tax, gratuity. Deposits typically non-refundable.
- **The constraint is tags, not animals** — elk quality around Luna (320–350"+, 16D record-book) rivals or beats the fenced comps; the disadvantage is *tag access structure*.
