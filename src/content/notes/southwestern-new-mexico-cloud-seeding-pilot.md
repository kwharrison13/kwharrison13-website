# Southwestern New Mexico Cloud Seeding Pilot — Reference & Playbook

**One-line:** *A single-file reference — science, viability, economics, regulatory path, proposal outline, and key contacts — for a winter cloud-seeding pilot over the Mogollon–Gila high country, framed around drought resilience and water supply (not fire suppression).*

A single-file reference covering the science, viability, economics, regulatory path,
proposal outline, and key contacts for a winter cloud-seeding pilot over the
Mogollon–Gila high country of southwestern New Mexico. Framed around **drought
resilience, watershed health, and reservoir storage**, with reduced fire spread as
a *secondary co-benefit* — explicitly **not** fire suppression.

> Companion files: `SW_NM_CloudSeeding_Pilot_Proposal.pdf` (the concept proposal) and
> `SW_NM_CloudSeeding_Contacts_CRM.csv` (importable contact list).

## 1. TL;DR

- Cloud seeding **enhances precipitation from clouds that already exist**; it cannot
  make rain from dry air. This single fact drives everything below.
- It is a **real, low-cost, operationally routine** water-management tool in the
  interior West — but a **modest-effect** one (~5–15% precipitation enhancement, often
  cited near 10%), and attribution is genuinely hard.
- Using it for **wildfire risk** is the wrong frame: it can't fight active fires and
  can't operate in the dry, cloudless conditions that drive peak fire danger. The
  **defensible frame is winter snowpack / drought / water-supply augmentation**, with
  reduced fire spread as a downstream co-benefit.
- The Gila/Mogollon setting is **less proven** than the tall northern-Rockies ranges
  where seeding has the best evidence, so the pilot must be **measurement-heavy**.
- Cost is not the obstacle: a ground-based pilot is plausibly **a few hundred K to
  ~$1M/year**; add aircraft + serious instrumentation and it's **$2–4M/year**.
- The obstacles are **regulatory/political**: NM permitting through the Interstate
  Stream Commission (ISC), an active anti–weather-modification public mood, and
  wilderness sensitivities.

## 2. The science, at a high level

Clouds are often "inefficient" — they hold supercooled liquid water or moisture that
never organizes into drops heavy enough to fall. Seeding nudges that process.

**Two methods:**

- **Glaciogenic (cold-cloud) seeding** — the dominant Western winter method. A trace
  amount of **silver iodide** (its crystal structure mimics ice) is released from
  ground generators or aircraft into clouds with supercooled liquid water, giving ice
  crystals something to form on; they grow heavy and fall as **additional snow**.
- **Hygroscopic (warm-cloud) seeding** — uses **salts** (sodium/potassium chloride) to
  give vapor something to condense around in warmer clouds; more typical for summer
  rain enhancement.

**Hard constraints:**

- **You need pre-existing seedable clouds.** No clouds → no seeding.
- **Effect is modest and hard to prove.** ~5–15% under favorable conditions; you can't
  run the unseeded counterfactual of the same storm. The 2017 SNOWIE experiment (Idaho)
  was a landmark because radar directly observed seeding signatures for the first time.
- **It cannot fight active fires**, and the driest, most fire-dangerous conditions are
  precisely when seeding is impossible.

## 3. Why "wildfire risk" is the hard framing

- **Peak Southwest fire danger is pre-monsoon (roughly May–June)** — the driest window,
  with essentially no seedable clouds. Seeding does nothing here.
- The only defensible mechanism is **indirect and slow**: seed in the wet seasons to
  build soil, fuel, and reservoir moisture over time, so fires that *do* start spread
  less readily.
- The region already has a powerful free version of this: **the summer monsoon is the
  natural "off switch"** for fire season. Seeding would marginally amplify a dominant
  natural signal — during the months you least need help.
- **Conclusion:** never pitch this as fire suppression. Pitch it as drought / water
  supply, with reduced fire spread as a secondary co-benefit.

## 4. Viability specific to the Gila / Mogollon

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Evidence base | **Weaker here** than tall winter-snowpack ranges (Wind Rivers, Wasatch, Sawtooths). Monsoon-dominated regime; thinner, less reliable winter snowpack. |
| Terrain | Real relief exists (Whitewater Baldy ~10,895 ft), which helps orographic seeding — but siting must be **upwind, outside wilderness**. |
| Wilderness | Gila Wilderness (est. 1924, first in the world) + Aldo Leopold Wilderness sit inside Gila NF. Don't place hardware inside; expect political friction advocating weather-mod over protected wilderness. |
| Regulatory | Defined but contested (see §6). Regulators are wary of seeding **near burn scars** due to flash-flood risk — fire and seeding interact badly, not helpfully. |
| Politics | Weather modification is a live, charged topic in NM right now, entangled with a national anti-"geoengineering" wave. |

## 5. Costs & economics

Cloud seeding is consistently **among the lowest-cost water-supply tools** — which is
why water managers use it.

**Whole-state annual operating budgets (benchmarks):**

| Program | Annual budget | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wyoming | ~$2.0M | Well-regarded; ~$8.8M over 5 yrs incl. ~$1.9M NCAR evaluation |
| Idaho | ~$3.9M | 57 remote + 25 manual generators, 3 aircraft, 24/7 meteorology |
| Utah | ~$9.2M | One of the largest; 170+ generators |

**Cost per unit of water:**

- Idaho: ~**$3.22 / acre-foot**
- Utah (2018): ~**$2.18 / acre-foot** for ~186,000 acre-feet (~12%) added, vs ~$20/AF
  for alternative supply.

**Scale points:**

- A modest single-basin ground-generator program can run a **few hundred K/year**
  (one eastern Idaho program: ~$311K for ~7% added snowpack).
- **Aircraft are the expensive line item;** ground generators are the cheap entry point.
- NM precedent: SOAR's 2022 eastern-NM season cost ~**$102,000** for a set of flights.
- Entire **US weather-mod industry is estimated at under $10M/year** — a small,
  relationship-driven field.

**Indicative pilot budget (from the proposal):**

| Phase | Scope | Duration | Indicative cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 0 — Feasibility & design | Climatology, siting, permitting support, evaluation plan | 3–6 months | $75K – $200K |
| Phase 1 — Ground-based pilot | Build + operate generator network, staffed each cold season, measurement | 2–3 cold seasons | $0.3M – $1.0M / yr |
| Phase 2 — Optional expansion | Added generators/aircraft, enhanced instrumentation + independent evaluation | As warranted | $2M – $4M / yr |

## 6. Regulatory & governance path (New Mexico)

- **Statute:** New Mexico **Weather Control Act**. Precipitation from seeding is treated
  as part of the natural water supply under existing water rights — creates no new
  appropriation.
- **Administrator:** the **Interstate Stream Commission (ISC)**, via its **Weather
  Control Committee**, under the Office of the State Engineer (OSE). Applications require
  ISC approval **with a public-comment period**.
- **Sponsor vehicle:** a local **Soil & Water Conservation District (SWCD)** — the
  established route (e.g., SOAR contracts with the Roosevelt County SWCD in the east).
- **Precedents / cautions:**
  - SOAR (Texas operator) licensed for eastern NM via Roosevelt SWCD.
  - ISC **excluded the Hermits Peak/Calf Canyon burn-scar area** from a prior approval
    over flood risk; ~23 public comments in opposition; a commissioner called for a more
    robust regulatory framework.
  - **Taos Pueblo** admonished the ISC over inadequate tribal consultation on a prior
    (rescinded) snowpack project — **tribal/government-to-government consultation matters.**
- **Funding politics:** a 2024 pilot bill (HB 130) had its ~$1.98M appropriation stripped
  to ~$1M in committee; 2025 "rainfall enhancement" bills (SB 471 / HB 362) proposed
  $3.6M. The topic stalled in the Senate in 2024 but has renewed interest into 2026.

## 7. The proposal (concept summary)

**Title:** Winter Snowpack and Soil-Moisture Augmentation Pilot — Upper Gila and
Mogollon Highlands.

**Dual audience:** (1) cloud-seeding operators to attract as partners; (2) state/local/
federal officials to petition for support. Kept general/high-level to serve both.

**Core design:**

- Winter (cold-season, ~Nov–Apr) **glaciogenic** seeding over Mogollon/Gila high terrain.
- **Primarily ground-based** generators sited upwind, **outside designated wilderness**;
  aircraft only as an optional later enhancement.
- **Suspension criteria** (flood/heavy-snow/public-safety) + **burn-scar exclusions**.
- **Measurement-heavy**: target-and-control comparison, snowpack/precip instrumentation,
  independent evaluator, environmental (silver) monitoring.
- **Sponsored by a local SWCD**; permitted through the ISC; transparent public reporting.

**Objectives:** primary = drought resilience, watershed/reservoir health, water-supply
reliability, and defensible regional evidence. Secondary = reduced fire spread (co-benefit,
not suppression).

**Honest limitations stated up front:** modest ~10% effect; requires clouds; attribution
is hard; public/regulatory sensitivity; trace silver iodide with monitoring.

## 10. Recommended sequencing

1. **Line up a sponsor** — approach San Francisco SWCD (Catron) and/or Grant SWCD.
2. **Engage the front door early** — ISC.WCComm@ose.nm.gov + Director Riseley-White;
   understand current application requirements and tribal-consultation expectations.
3. **Secure a legislative champion** — Terrazas (district) and Brantley (region) as
   leads; Chatfield/Block as proven cloud-seeding votes.
4. **Bring in an operator** — start with SOAR (already NM-fluent) and NAWC (winter
   snowpack pedigree) for feasibility scoping; keep evaluation independent (e.g., DRI).
5. **Coordinate federal siting** — Gila NF Supervisor's Office for generator placement
   and wilderness exclusions.
6. **Assemble multi-year funding** — state appropriation + Bureau of Reclamation
   cost-share + downstream-beneficiary contributions.

## 11. Key caveats to keep front-of-mind

- Frame as **water supply / drought**, never fire suppression.
- Expect a **~10% effect**, provable only across multiple wet/dry/average years — so
  **commit multi-year** and **measure independently**.
- **Wilderness and burn scars are exclusion zones**, not targets.
- **Tribal consultation** is a real procedural requirement, not a formality.
- The **public/political climate** around weather modification in NM is currently charged.
