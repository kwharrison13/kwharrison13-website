# Consulting Firm For Creatives

> 💡 **Ideation-stage idea** — one of Kyle's [Entity Ideas](/notes/entity-ideas) business concepts, captured from his Roam notebook. Not an operating company; preserved here so the idea (and its research) survives.

## Idea capture (from Roam)

- I posted this question in several Facebook groups to see what people thought:
- My brother and I both do photography but he is also going to law school and he brought up an interesting idea about using his law degree to provide legal counsel/services to creative professionals (photographers, videographers, musicians, etc.) Maybe its something that would be more for learning amateurs since I'm sure lawyers are happy to handle really big projects. What kind of response would you have to that? I've seen a lot of issues on pages like this with clients causing problems, going back on deals, etc. Its just an idea, but what are your thoughts?
- Here are some of the best answers I got:
- I am also enrolled in Law school here in Australia. That's an interesting idea, especially since the smaller players are constantly stung by businesses who are not very honest with a lot of things. I think it's a good idea, since I don't think it's market that is targeted. Of the top of my head, I'm thinking of targeting schools and students to use your services ( template forms they pay for once and quite cheaply) and then if things go pear-shaped, hopefully they'll come back to you to enforce the legality of the contracts. The great thing about making it affordable, is that schools generally try to get the students to do the right thing and the you'll get new clients each year, and build up good will with graduates, same reason why Adobe makes the student memberships so affordable.
- My husband is a lawyer and I'd say it could be a good thing to know, but unfortunately, I don't think many photographers would not be able to pay the typical hourly rates. Which would, in turn, not be beneficial to the attorney. It certainly wouldn't be a field you could bank on.
- I agree with Erin. The need is definitely there, but I don't feel the market as a whole would be able or willing to pay normal legal rates. Most photographers just don't charge enough to have those funds available.
- He could work for PPA. Seriously, nice idea but most 'creative professionals' don't have the money for something like that IMO
- There's a market for something like that, for sure. But the problem with it is that the law is so different from place to place. You either have to have specialized information for different areas, or only market to creatives in your normal area.
- [Rachel Brenke - Business Consultant & Photographer](https://www.facebook.com/RachelBrenke2) she is also a photog, lawyer, and [SnapSpace Studios](https://www.facebook.com/pages/SnapSpace-Studios/310136439138557) owner
- I think that would be good! I know a lot of people need their contracts looked over (if he is able to do that)
- I think it would be very helpful to have someone with industry knowledge provide legal advice. I've met with an accountant who knew little to nothing about our industry and I found the experience frustrating. I asked a lawyer friend to review and revise my contract for me - but I was the one who drafted it and put photography specific clauses in there since I didn't expect him to be in the know. He did have questions for me about some things and reworded some sections to be less vague (read: greater protection for me) but I think marrying the two subjects together would be awesome!
- I know UVU offers a full class based on essentially "Covering your butt in contracts" talking about the legal ramifications of contacts between people offering creative services and their clients. I think it's a big deal, but trying to use his law degree in such a niche market wouldn't work in my opinion. A lot of photographers, videographers, musicians etc are already stereotyped as bottom of the barrel, barely making it, waiting tables on the side kind of people, they don't have money to be spending on law advice. Again, my personal opinion is I'd rather spend money upgrading my camera equipment and eat a bad contract or two then spend money making sure I get the most out of all my contracts.
- Yes, you can find one and yes people are lazy and won't do their homework, BUT if you are relying on your knowledge of the law and someone ELSE's contract without having it reviewed by a lawyer, then you might be the one who is surprised to find out you aren't as "covered" as you think.
- The Musicians Union supports a lot of musicians that are signed up to them, if there are any disputes, the MU will often have their back and with strong legal support, they also provide template contracts which are pretty good. But I'm sure a lot of amateurs would appreciate help when they're not sure where they stand legally
- Honestly people are lazy who don't do the basic research on contracts. Sorry but even the simplest business can find all the info they need. You need to be firm and clear in your contract. Cover yourself AND the client so there is no issue. I wouldn't pay any one legal to do something that is so dependent on my needs as a business
- My sister-in-law is a photographer and a lawyer as well. Being able to call her and get her advice has been a true blessing! If I didn't have her, I wouldn't mind paying small one-time fees to a lawyer who knows the industry.
- I know for certain if he could draw up some good legal contract templates (general and then for specific states) it would be "one and done" and then he could get residual income by charging on etsy or offering maybe an hour consult time with each purchase or something?
- The link above is Rachel brenke's website. She has done a workshop on creativeLIVE, written a book about how to run a business, she is a business consultant and a lawyer. She sells awesome contracts just for photographers (I own a set). There is a niche for this and she pretty much is the only person doing it.
- There is a whole field of law practitioners just for creative professionals, but it couldn't hurt to join them. You should always look up a performing rights/copyright lawyer when you're signing a big contract, all my professors have always recommended it being worth the money. Harvard business school has a free law services program for new professional musicians called the Recording Artists Project, "RAP", they came and lectured at my college a couple times while I was there.
- The Musicians Union supports a lot of musicians that are signed up to them, if there are any disputes, the MU will often have their back and with strong legal support, they also provide template contracts which are pretty good. But I'm sure a lot of amateurs would appreciate help when they're not sure where they stand legally
- I use lawyers mostly for these three things: writing contracts, cease and desist on intellectual property infringements and then enforcing contracts here and there. Rarely litigation if ever. The other three usually end up avoiding the need for the last one.
