# Acequias

**One-line:** *Gravity-fed community irrigation ditches — the oldest European-derived water institution in what is now the United States — that organize water as a shared community resource, brought from Spain via the Rio Grande valley.*

A durable, reusable water-institution concept surfaced in [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county). Relevant to [The Luna Landing](/notes/the-luna-landing)'s land/water thinking: the acequia is a living model of communal, rights-and-obligations water governance in exactly Luna's region.

## How sources describe it

- **Community resource, not private allocation.** A *mayordomo* (ditch boss) allocates flows; every irrigator with rights owes annual labor on the *limpieza y saca* (spring cleaning of the mother ditch) (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- **Oldest European-derived water institution in the U.S.**, brought from Spain by way of the Rio Grande valley; farmed the San Francisco valley bottomland from the 1870s "Frisco" plazas onward (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- **Formal legal status:** under later New Mexico law, acequias became **political subdivisions of the state**; several still operate on the San Francisco River and its tributaries today (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
