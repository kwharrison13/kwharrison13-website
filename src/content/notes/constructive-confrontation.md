# Constructive Confrontation

**One-line definition:** *Intel's term, coined by Andy Grove, for a style of "ferociously arguing with one another while remaining friends" — developed out of the company's early struggles with manufacturing problems.* — [Only the Paranoid Survive](/books/only-the-paranoid-survive)

## How sources describe it

- "We became good at solving problems. We became highly focused on tangible results (our word for it is 'output'). And from all the early bickering, we developed a style of ferociously arguing with one another while remaining friends (we call this 'constructive confrontation')." ([Only the Paranoid Survive](/books/only-the-paranoid-survive))
- Grove connects it to a Charlie Munger paraphrase he attaches to his chief technologist's handling of the RISC/CISC debate: "I don't feel like I deserve to hold an opinion unless I can state the opposite of that opinion as well as the people who actually disagree with me." Debates are likened to a photographer sharpening contrast when developing a print — clarifying each side's argument rather than forcing unanimous agreement.
- The precondition Grove names: "constructively debating tough issues and getting somewhere is only possible when people can speak their minds without fear of punishment."

## Where it shows up

- [Only the Paranoid Survive](/books/only-the-paranoid-survive) — traced to Intel's early manufacturing struggles and applied later to strategic debates like RISC vs. CISC.
