# Witnessing the Beginning of A Chemical Empire

**Author:** [Tobias Grether-Murray](/notes/tobias-grether-murray)
**URL:** https://medium.com/@tobias_gm/witnessing-the-beginning-of-a-chemical-empire-a11b0eb8c561
**One-line:** *Five lessons from a summer 2023 internship at [Solugen](/notes/solugen) on how to build a deeptech company — a "child of both [Y Combinator](/notes/y-combinator) and MIT."*

## Key claims

- **Make something people want, even in chemicals.** Solugen's founders interviewed failed Cleantech 1.0 founders and found the #1 cause of failure was too much focus on the technology instead of the customer's problem — you need technical founders who love the technology but don't love it *for its own sake*.
- **In deeptech you make deep, hard-to-reverse bets.** Unlike software, you can't "just fix a bug" — millions get sunk into reactors, lab space, and feedstock before a pivot is even possible, which makes team quality and decision quality matter more, not less.
- **You hire talent you "don't deserve" through storytelling and visible progress**, not through salary — an exciting, hard mission plus proof of momentum is what attracts exceptional builders before the product itself can.
- **Reality-distortion-field goal-setting compounds.** Citing [Steve Jobs](/notes/steve-jobs) and [Elon Musk](/notes/elon-musk) (a Tesla engineer's story about insisting on one motor instead of two), the essay argues that setting impossibly ambitious deadlines — even when missed — still outperforms realistic ones; Solugen targeted a 6-month plant build, missed it, and still built in 2 years what would normally take 10.
- **Seek [Company-City Fit](/notes/company-city-fit).** Solugen's Houston location is a deliberate bet on engineering talent density, a regional cleantech boom, energy/water supply, zoning, and logistics — "where?" makes "how?" easier.
- **Deeptech marketing means attracting talent and owning the niche**, not television ads — founders worth getting behind, word of mouth, podcasts, and industry appearances (SynBioBeta, SXSW) build a brand that enzymologists recognize even if the public never sees it.

## Notable quotes

> "If you were given all of the technology, you would still lose to Solugen." — on the team's decision-making as the real moat

> "This has never happened at Solugen precisely because of their emphasis on 'doing no harm.'" — on the plant's safety record

> "For every deeptech company being built, one should ask the question of 'where?' because it will make answering the question of 'how?' a lot easier."

> "To know and not to do is not to know." — Stephen Covey, quoted in the conclusion

## How it connects

- [Solugen](/notes/solugen) — the company itself; this essay is the wiki's only current source on it.
- [Company-City Fit](/notes/company-city-fit) — the concept named directly in the essay's fifth lesson, with Houston as the worked example.
- [Elon Musk](/notes/elon-musk) / [Steve Jobs](/notes/steve-jobs) — invoked as the "reality distortion field" exemplars behind Solugen's compressed plant-build timeline.
- [Y Combinator](/notes/y-combinator) — Solugen is described as "a child of both YCombinator and MIT," pairing Silicon Valley company-building instincts with a 500-year-old industry.
