# Disagree and Commit

**One-line definition:** *Kyle's shorthand label for Grove's account of how a debate resolves — not into unanimous agreement, but into a decision that all sides then commit to, having sharpened their arguments in the arguing.* — [Only the Paranoid Survive](/books/only-the-paranoid-survive)

## How sources describe it

- Grove: "Don't think for a moment that at the end of such debates all participants will arrive at a unanimous point of view. That's naive. However, through the process of presenting their own opinions, the participants will refine their own arguments and facts so that they are in much clearer focus... Debates are like the process through which a photographer sharpens the contrast when developing a print." Kyle's inline note on this passage reads "Disagree and commit." ([Only the Paranoid Survive](/books/only-the-paranoid-survive))
- Grove ties commitment itself to decisiveness once a direction is chosen: "Put all your eggs in one basket and WATCH THAT BASKET" — hedging dilutes commitment and confuses the organization; most companies die not because they were wrong but because they wouldn't commit.

## Where it shows up

- [Only the Paranoid Survive](/books/only-the-paranoid-survive) — Kyle's label for the resolution phase following [Constructive Confrontation](/notes/constructive-confrontation).

## Related concepts

- [Constructive Confrontation](/notes/constructive-confrontation) — the debate phase that precedes commitment.
- [Strategic Dissonance](/notes/strategic-dissonance) — what happens when a company fails to actually commit after debating.
