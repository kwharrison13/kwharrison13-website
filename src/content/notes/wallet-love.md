# Wallet Love

> 💡 **Ideation-stage idea** — one of Kyle's [Entity Ideas](/notes/entity-ideas) business concepts, captured from his Roam notebook. Not an operating company; preserved here so the idea (and its research) survives.

## Idea capture (from Roam)

- All right, I wasn't able to find the mission statement I made for it, but I was able to find a couple of spreadsheets I made for it. One is just calculating basic costs and revenue and the other shows the break down of the cards and how the money is transferred.
- To quickly explain how it would work: There are two sides of the website. One side is donating to a charity of your choice through donating unused gift cards. There are many statistics on how billions of dollars of gift card cash go unused every year. This side of the website would would show an address and sheet to fill out and mail with the unused gift cards they want to donate. On that sheet they fill out they choose a charity of their choice. There would be a list of 100 major charities to choose from.
- The second section of the website would be donating through buying gift cards. They would click on that side of the website and there would be lists of available gift cards to purchase. They would choose one, choose a charity, and we would mail them their gift card. The gift cards put on that section of the website are also marked down a percentage. So you would get a 20$ gift card for 19.20, for example.
- How this is able to work is through re-selling the gift cards we receive. So let's say we get 5 gift cards for Banana Republic that each have 2$ on them. We keep track of a percentage of each of the two dollar gift cards to go to the charity of the donator's choice as an IOU until we receive the funds from reselling the card. We take those gift cards to BR and trade them in for one, new 10$ gift card for re-sale. We sell it for 9.40 (not an exact number). A percentage of that 9.40 goes to a charity of the buyer's choice as well.
- In the end about 5% is given to the buyer of the card as an incentive to buy the card below value. Another percentage of the cards donated is given to the charity of the donator's choice, and another percentage is given to the charity of the buyer's choice. Wallet Love would retain about 30% of the funds from the card.
- Here are my concerns with this. At first I was planning on this being a sole proprietorship, but in Utah you can only get tax deductions on the first 50% of revenue that you donate to charities. Because I plan on donating much more that 50%, we would be getting taxed in income we no longer have.
- The other option was to become a non-profit. However, the more I researched the application and talked to people who have run non-profits or worked with non-profits, I realized this business plan would not qualify. Non-profits need a central goal or issue they are trying to solve. This plan is to donate to any non-profit that already exists. The only unique goal is how the funds are taken, which I don't believe would count.
- Another idea would be to do the sole-proprietorship, but wait a while to donate the funds, instead putting them in some investment and have them gain interest until I could donate the amount needed and still take in enough revenue to pay the taxes and still benefit. But at that point my expertise fails me. I know very little on investing.
- And the question still remains if people would donate unused gift cards. I know it works for locks of love, I mean who would have thought so many women would willingly cut off 10 inches of un-damaged hair to donate. But I don't know how gift cards would work. I get mixed responses from asking people their opinion.
- For these reasons I have put Wallet Love on the back burner, but I'd be very interested in your dad's thoughts if you feel you want to pass the idea on to him.
