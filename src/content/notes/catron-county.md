# Catron County

**One-line:** *The largest county in New Mexico by area (~7,000 sq mi) and one of its emptiest (~3,500–3,800 residents) — the county [Luna](/notes/luna-new-mexico) sits in, in the state's southwestern corner near the Arizona line.*

The **containing place** for [Luna, New Mexico](/notes/luna-new-mexico) and the geographic frame for the [The Luna Landing](/notes/the-luna-landing) program's land / water / tourism research. Where county-level context (hydrology, forests, governance, economy) aggregates.

## What we know

- **Largest NM county by area** (~7,000 sq mi, bigger than four U.S. states) and one of the emptiest (~3,500–3,800 residents) (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- The **Continental Divide** runs through it, splitting it between the Lower Colorado River Basin (west — [Gila](/notes/gila), San Francisco, Tularosa rivers) and the Rio Grande Basin (east), plus closed interior basins like the Plains of San Agustin (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- **Reserve** is the county seat (grew out of the 1870s "Frisco" / San Francisco Plaza Hispanic settlements); the name "Reserve" comes from the surrounding forest reserves — Gila, Apache, Cibola national forests (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- Settled in three overlapping 1880s waves — **mining** (Mogollon, Cooney), **Hispanic acequia farming**, and **Mormon settlement** at [Luna](/notes/luna-new-mexico) (1883); **ranching** became and remains the dominant land use (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- **Water-constrained and drought-prone**: structural scarcity, an interstate surface-water cap since 1964, and the 2000–present [Southwestern Megadrought](/notes/southwestern-megadrought) — see [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county) for the full 140-year history.
