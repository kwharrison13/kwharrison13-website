# Answers: Straightforward Answers to Tough Gospel Questions

A book by [Joseph Fielding McConkie](/notes/joseph-fielding-mcconkie) that Kyle cites in his [Come, Follow Me](/notes/come-follow-me) study of Alma 13–16 (June 15–21, 2020. Alma 13–16: "Enter into the Rest of the Lord"). It surfaces under the thread on [Women and the Priesthood](/notes/women-and-the-priesthood), alongside a quotation from [Spencer W. Kimball](/notes/spencer-w-kimball) about faithful women receiving foreordained assignments in the premortal world — used as a reference point for the question of women's relationship to priesthood and foreordination.

**Context:** *Answers: Straightforward Answers to Tough Gospel Questions* is a question-and-answer volume by Latter-day Saint author and BYU religion professor Joseph Fielding McConkie, addressing doctrinal and historical questions members commonly raise.

## Highlights — [Women and the Priesthood](/notes/women-and-the-priesthood)

Verbatim highlights and Q&A from the book's chapter on women and the priesthood, as captured in Kyle's study:

- The men had their turn at the Council of Nicea, at which philosophical speculation replaced the plain meaning of scripture, Christ became his own Father, and the gospel plan was transformed into an incomprehensible mystery. **It could be argued that in fairness women ought now to have their chance to create a god and a theology too.**
- The difficulty is certainly apparent: when everyone in the ward has obtained perfect equality in authority, there is no longer any authority. Every man, woman, and child has become a bishop unto him or herself.

**Question: Do we learn any distinctive or important truths about the role of women from the restored gospel?**
- The woman, the Lord said, was to be his "help meet"; without her the man has neither the power of creation nor the hope of salvation. (D&C 131:1-4)
- Where it has been the propensity of men to rule by strength, it has been that of women to change the destiny of nations by the greater power of love.

**Question: The doctrine of the Church holds that spiritual gifts and the keys to the mysteries of the kingdom are accessible through the Melchizedek Priesthood. Are women less eligible than men to exercise spiritual gifts and to comprehend the mysteries of the kingdom?**
- The right to possess spiritual gifts is no more a matter of gender than is the right to exercise faith or to repent or to be baptized.
- D&C 130:20-21 — There is a law, irrevocably decreed in heaven before the foundations of this world, upon which all blessings are predicated—And when we obtain any blessing from God, it is by obedience to that law upon which it is predicated.
- 2 Nephi 26:33 — he inviteth them all to come unto him and partake of his goodness; and he denieth none that come unto him, black and white, bond and free, male and female... and all are alike unto God, both Jew and Gentile.
- Alma 32:23 — And now, he imparteth his word by angels unto men, yea, not only men but women also. Now this is not all; little children do have words given unto them many times, which confound the wise and the learned.
- D&C 84:19-22 — this greater priesthood administereth the gospel and holdeth the key of the mysteries of the kingdom... in the ordinances thereof, the power of godliness is manifest... for without this no man can see the face of God, even the Father, and live.
- The ordinances of the priesthood out of which "the power of godliness" comes bring the same promise of blessings to women that they do to men.

**Question: Was it necessary for the [Relief Society](/notes/relief-society) to be organized before women could receive the temple endowment? Could there be more to the Relief Society than most members understand?**

**Question: If women cannot hold the [Priesthood](/notes/priesthood), how can they have authority to function in their Church callings?** For instance, by what authority do female temple ordinance workers perform ordinances?

**Question: How do we explain women giving blessings to the sick in Nauvoo?**
- "It is no sin for anybody to administer that has faith, or if the sick have faith to be healed by their administration." — [Joseph Smith](/notes/joseph-smith) (History of the Church 4:604)

**Question: Why can a man be sealed to more than one wife but a woman can't be sealed to more than one man?**

**Question: How will women who do not marry and have children in mortality spend eternity?**

**Question: How much individual life and personality is a wife and mother "allowed" to have? How can one determine when it is all right to put her own needs first? Or is it never all right?**
- Significantly, those days in which we don't have time for prayer are the days in which we have the greatest need for prayer. Similarly, those days in which there is no time to eat or rest or find a brief spiritual respite are the days in which those things are most needed. **These are matters on which the needs of the mother must come first, simply because even the best mothers cannot share what they do not possess.**

**Question: Is it better for a woman to remain single all her life than to marry outside the Church?**
- It is hard to suppose that someone with a meaningful understanding of the gospel would marry outside the faith. Those who have done so have often found that their posterity also end up out of the Church.
