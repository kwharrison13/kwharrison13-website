# Prior Appropriation

**One-line:** *The Western U.S. water-rights doctrine of "first in time, first in right" — the oldest claims hold seniority and get their full allocation before junior rights get any, when the water runs short.*

A durable, reusable water-law concept surfaced in [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county). The rule that governs who actually gets wet water in a dry year across the [Gila](/notes/gila) / San Francisco system — and a key constraint for any [The Luna Landing](/notes/the-luna-landing) land/water use.

## How sources describe it

- **"First in time, first in right"** — the oldest ditches and claims (many dating to the 1880s in [Catron County](/notes/catron-county)) hold seniority when the river runs short (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- Operates *underneath* the 1964 interstate ceiling from *Arizona v. California*: entitlement on paper vs. wet water in the channel. The oldest [Acequias](/notes/acequias) and diversions are the senior rights (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- **Beneficial use** is the touchstone of New Mexico water law — a right must be put to actual beneficial use; speculative, unused claims fail (the basis for rejecting the Augustin Plains export scheme) (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
