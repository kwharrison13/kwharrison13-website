# Calvin and Hobbes

[Bill Watterson](/notes/bill-watterson)'s comic strip, cited by [Naval Ravikant](/notes/naval-ravikant) in [The Almanack of Naval Ravikant](/books/the-almanack-of-naval-ravikant) ("Escape competition through authenticity") as the go-to example of a creative work so bound up with its creator's identity that no one could out-compete it: "Is someone going to compete with Bill Watterson and create a better Calvin and Hobbes? No. They're being authentic." It's paired with [Dilbert](/notes/dilbert) ([Scott Adams](/notes/scott-adams)) in the same passage — a one-line illustration of authenticity as an unassailable moat, not further discussed.

- [The Almanack of Naval Ravikant](/books/the-almanack-of-naval-ravikant) — Naval's authenticity-as-moat argument.
