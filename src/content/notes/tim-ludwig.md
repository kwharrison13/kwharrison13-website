# Tim Ludwig

**One-line:** *Investor at [Ohana Capital](/notes/ohana-capital); works on debt-financed SMB roll-ups and [ESOP](/notes/esop)-style employee-ownership models — served as a reference call on [Teamshares](/notes/teamshares), comparing it to Canada's Link Equity.*

On the March 2020 [Teamshares](/notes/teamshares) reference call he described Link Equity's playbook — won't buy more than 4x [EBITDA](/notes/ebitda), raises debt from Canadian retirees at an ~8% coupon and cross-collateralizes the businesses to offer exposure to income-producing SMBs, repaying over 8–10 years, then expanding across geographies (Canada → Denmark). His written feedback on the [Teamshares](/notes/teamshares) deck flagged the risks he cared most about: **management-bench risk** (no real bench of talent in small companies; the danger of 2+ CEOs failing at once), **return underwriting** (constantly selling your winners caps upside), the unusual **50% seller-debt** financing at above-market rates, the questionable slide-18 assumption of 10–15-year money at 8–10%, and the hazard of extrapolating from an anomalous economic window.
