# Luna, New Mexico

**One-line:** *Kyle's family town — an unincorporated community of ~150 on the New Mexico–Arizona border, settled by [Mormon](/notes/mormon) ranchers in 1883 and home to five generations of Kyle's family.*

The shared **place page** for the [The Luna Landing](/notes/the-luna-landing) program — everything about the town itself (history, geography, current status) hangs here so the venture and history pages can link in rather than restate it.

## What we know

- **Population ~150**, on the New Mexico–Arizona border (source: [Family Towns](/essays/family-towns)).
- **Named after [Solomon Luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Luna)**, at one point the wealthiest man in New Mexico — but **settled by Mormon ranchers in 1883** (source: [Family Towns](/essays/family-towns)).
- **[William Laney Jr.](/notes/william-laney-jr)**, Kyle's great-great-great grandfather, was among those 1883 settlers — born in [Salt Lake City](/notes/salt-lake) in 1848, asked at age 35 to move with a group of Saints to settle in New Mexico (source: [Family Towns](/essays/family-towns)). Per Kyle's organization brief, he was sent by [Wilford Woodruff](/notes/wilford-woodruff) to help settle Luna.
- **Five generations** of Laneys / Thompsons / Shupes have lived in Luna since; the family still owns property there, which Kyle will inherit (source: [Family Towns](/essays/family-towns), organization brief).
- Kyle's daughter is named **Eve Luna Harrison** (source: [Family Towns](/essays/family-towns)).
- Kyle frames Luna as the anchor of his [City Building](/notes/city-building) portfolio idea and wants "to find ways to help it thrive" — the seed of [The Luna Landing](/notes/the-luna-landing) (source: [Family Towns](/essays/family-towns)).

## Water & setting

Luna sits in the western reaches of [Catron County](/notes/catron-county) — the largest, emptiest county in New Mexico — near the Arizona line, in the Lower Colorado / [Gila](/notes/gila) drainage. Water is the binding constraint on the town's viability, then and now.

- The 1883 **Luna Valley colony** was founded when the Swapp brothers (William, John, Melvin) and other Latter-day Saints purchased a large land claim; settlers built a fort against raiders and cattle barons (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- They dug the **Luna Irrigation Ditch** to water the valley floor from **Luna Creek and the upper San Francisco drainage** — its historic alignment was still being archaeologically documented a century later (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- Luna exemplifies the county's essential constraint: viable farming was possible only where a reliable stream could be diverted onto a pocket of level, arable land — and those pockets were few and small (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- The region is now in the driest stretch in 1,200+ years — see [Southwestern Megadrought](/notes/southwestern-megadrought) and the full [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county) history (interstate surface-water cap, the failed Gila diversion, the Augustin Plains export fight, the 2026 drought emergency).

## The family land

- Two generations of Laneys patented **~208 acres** in and around Section 5, T6S R20W — right at the Luna townsite on the San Francisco River: [William Laney Jr.](/notes/william-laney-jr) (~146.5 ac, 1892) and his son [William Sheppard Laney](/notes/william-sheppard-laney) (~61.8 ac, 1916). **None is held by a Laney today** — it passed to unrelated owners and family trusts. Full record: [Laney Family Land at Luna](/notes/laney-family-land-at-luna).
- The market for land in and around Luna, and the elk-tag ([E-PLUS](/notes/e-plus)) economics of owning it, are analyzed in E-PLUS Land Strategy — Luna and Luna Landing Retreat — Master Reference.

## Current status

- **Unincorporated** (~194 people). A workstream of [The Luna Landing](/notes/the-luna-landing) is moving the town toward becoming an [Incorporated Village](/notes/incorporated-village) — see [Municipal Incorporation (NM)](/notes/municipal-incorporation-nm). The town has an eighteen-year track record of organizing against outside capital (source: E-PLUS Land Strategy — Luna) — a live constraint on any land-assembly play.
