# Bill Watterson

Cartoonist, creator of *[Calvin and Hobbes](/notes/calvin-and-hobbes)*, whom [Naval Ravikant](/notes/naval-ravikant) names as an example of authenticity as a competitive moat. In [The Almanack of Naval Ravikant](/books/the-almanack-of-naval-ravikant)'s "escape competition through authenticity" argument, Naval pairs him with [Scott Adams](/notes/scott-adams) ([Dilbert](/notes/dilbert)): "Is someone going to compete with Bill Watterson and create a better Calvin and Hobbes? No." The point is that work which is a genuine extension of who its maker is can't be out-competed. It's a passing example illustrating the argument, not a discussion of Watterson himself.

## Where this appears

- [The Almanack of Naval Ravikant](/books/the-almanack-of-naval-ravikant) — Naval's authenticity-as-moat argument.
- [Dilbert](/notes/dilbert) — the sibling example ([Scott Adams](/notes/scott-adams)) named in the same passage.
