# ARK Invest

**One-line definition:** *Cathie Wood's research-forward, disruptive-innovation asset manager — a [Wordsmith](/notes/wordsmiths) "think in public" culture organized around publicly publishing its models and theses.*

## How it shows up

Two very different readings of ARK run through Kyle's research:

- **As a [Wordsmith](/notes/wordsmiths) culture** (The Storytelling of Investing - Research) — ARK is held up for treating accuracy as the proof of understanding: *"Being able to describe something accurately and briefly is an indicator that you understand it more holistically. But 'accurately' is the trick."* Since its earliest days ARK has published research publicly (e.g. the 2017 Bitcoin white paper co-written with Coinbase), tuning its output to the world's "information engine."
- **As a cautionary tale** (The Gospel of ARK Invest - Research, behind the essay [The Gospel of ARK Invest](/essays/the-gospel-of-ark-invest)) — compelling on paper, ridiculous in execution. ARK operates as ETFs rather than a hedge fund, drawing capital from **individual contributors to the ETF rather than institutional investors** (its "democratized" framing), and charges ~0.75% of AUM with **no performance fee** — so it earns fees on inflows regardless of performance. Kyle's critique: the incentive is inflows, not returns, and the calls read as **"dazzle over diligence"** ([Disruptive Innovation](/notes/disruptive-innovation) as the load-bearing, overloaded term; Wright's Law as the modeling crutch). ARKK ran up in Covid, then fell from ~$27B (early 2021) toward ~$7B — much of it individual investors' wealth — yet still pulled net inflows even in 2022.
