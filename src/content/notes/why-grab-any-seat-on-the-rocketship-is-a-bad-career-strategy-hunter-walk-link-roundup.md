# Why Grab Any Seat on the Rocketship Is a Bad Career Strategy (Hunter Walk Link Roundup)

**Author:** [Hunter Walk](/notes/hunter-walk)
**URL:** https://hunterwalk.com/2026/08/02/why-grab-any-seat-on-the-rocketship-is-a-bad-career-strategy-this-vc-thinks-people-are-right-to-hate-todays-capitalism-looks-for-founders-who-play-rts-games-and-a-chrome-extension-which-block/
**One-line:** *Walk's weekly "Hot August URLs" link roundup — five short items on career strategy, the creator economy, capitalism critique, an anti-slop shopping tool, and what post-agentic founders look like.*

## Key claims

- **["Picking Rocketships Is a Bad Career Strategy"](https://open.substack.com/pub/mollyg/p/the-new-rocketship-test?r=2c7b&utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&utm_medium=web) [Molly Graham](/notes/molly-graham)** — counters [Eric Schmidt](/notes/eric-schmidt)'s famous advice to Sheryl Sandberg ("when you're offered a seat on a rocketship, you don't ask which seat, you just get on") with a different litmus test: *"If this company struggles or fails, will I still be glad I took the job?"* A good career decision isn't just about financial outcome — it's about the lessons, people, experience, and reputation effect, because narratives and company trajectories can change quickly.
- **["The Creator Economy Has a New Middle Class"](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/how-tiktok-creators-earn-midlevel-salaries-with-small-audiences-brand-deals?utm_source=website&utm_medium=share&utm_campaign=copy) [Melos Ambaye/Bloomberg]** — a mix of monetization streams (affiliate links, brand deals, loyal fans) lets creators beyond the top of the pyramid earn at/above standard-job salaries. It's still a hustle and most fall well short of top-creator income, but the fun implication is that creators can "niche up" into areas without prior pop-culture-level awareness (dentistry, accounting, etc.).
- **["Why People Hate Capitalism"](https://open.substack.com/pub/vijaypande/p/why-people-hate-capitalism?r=2c7b&utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&utm_medium=web) [Vijay Pande](/notes/vijay-pande)** — an entrepreneur-turned-VC argues capitalism has become captive to entrenched rent-seekers who pull opportunity away from the individual; AI will kill some, but not all, of those "rackets."
- **[Knockoff](https://knockoff.co/)** — a Chrome extension that filters "trademark-squat pseudo-brands" out of Amazon search and flags mass-market catalog stock passed off as Etsy "handmade" listings, leaving only "brands with a reputation to lose" (see also [Fast Company's coverage](https://www.fastcompany.com/91570721/amazon-shopping-slop-viral-new-tool-filters-out-knockoff-brands)).
- **["The Post-Agentic Founder"](https://open.substack.com/pub/digitalnative/p/the-post-agentic-founder?r=2c7b&utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&utm_medium=web) [Rex Woodbury](/notes/rex-woodbury)** — Woodbury's list of traits he looks for in this wave of founders. The one Walk highlights: the best post-agentic founders resemble strong RTS/chess players — they maintain a model of the whole board, allocate scarce resources, sequence actions, and orchestrate many pieces (and, increasingly, many agents) toward one objective.

## Notable quotes

> "My ultimate litmus test for a job is: If this company struggles or fails, will I still be glad I took the job?" — [Molly Graham](/notes/molly-graham)

> "Capitalism has become captive to entrenched rent seekers, which pull opportunity away from the individual." — [Vijay Pande](/notes/vijay-pande) (Walk's paraphrase of Pande's thesis)

> "They win by maintaining a model of the entire board, allocating scarce resources, sequencing actions, and directing many pieces toward a unified objective. Post-agentic founders do the same." — [Rex Woodbury](/notes/rex-woodbury), on founders who are good at RTS games
