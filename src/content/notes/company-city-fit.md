# Company-City Fit

Finding the right city for a startup to succeed — treated, in [Witnessing the Beginning of A Chemical Empire](/notes/witnessing-the-beginning-of-a-chemical-empire), as an especially crucial decision for deeptech companies, where a plant, its supply chain, and its talent pool are all physically anchored to one place in a way a software company's aren't. The essay's example is [Solugen](/notes/solugen)'s deliberate choice of Houston: a high density of chemical engineers and biochemists, a Houston/Austin cleantech boom, abundant clean energy and water supply, chemical-plant-friendly zoning, and strong shipping/delivery routes. The framing (heard by the author in a public interview with a Solugen co-founder) is that for every deeptech company, answering "where?" makes answering "how?" a lot easier.

**Context:** the term riffs on "product-market fit," extending the fit concept from what you build to where you build it — a consideration for [City Building](/notes/city-building) as much as for company strategy.
