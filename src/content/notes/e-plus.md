# E-PLUS

**One-line:** *New Mexico's Elk Private Land Use System — NMDGF issues private-land elk tags ("authorizations") to qualifying landowners by an acreage × habitat formula; landowners may use, sell, barter, or trade them. The only way to sell a hunter a guaranteed elk tag in a state with no preference points.*

A durable, high-value concept for the [Luna Hunting Economy](/notes/luna-hunting-economy) — the mechanism the owned-hunts/lodge venture hinges on. The deep strategy analysis lives in E-PLUS Land Strategy — Luna; this page is the reusable definition.

## How it works

- **The formula (NMAC 19.30.5):** a ranch's **deeded acres × Elk Contribution Rating (ECR)** = ranch-weighted acres; divided by **total private deeded acres in the GMU primary zone** = the ranch's percent share; × the GMU's private-land authorization pool = authorizations per ranch (source: E-PLUS Land Strategy — Luna).
- **Zero-sum within a GMU.** Your share is your weighted acres over *every* private acre in the unit — adding land shrinks every neighbor's share. (The political landmine in [Luna](/notes/luna-new-mexico).)
- **ECR — the only base multiplier**, three settings: Occasional 1.00 / Frequent 1.25 / Continuous 1.50. Driven by elk presence, which is driven by **water and forage** — so river-bottom irrigated meadow can rate 1.5 (a ~50% acreage premium; the cash value of Luna's water advantage).
- **Base ranch vs. SCR.** A ranch clearing ≥1 whole authorization is a **base ranch** (guaranteed annual allocation). Below that it's a **small contributing ranch (SCR)** — capped at one authorization via a weighted lottery, *unless* it completes an NMDGF-approved **Habitat Improvement Project** (the only route past the cap).
- **Ranch-Only vs. Unit-Wide.** Unit-wide (Primary Management Zone only) lets the tag hunt all public land in the GMU + enrolled private — a key to ~1M acres in a public-land-heavy unit. The prize; elect unit-wide.
- **The public/private split** of a GMU's elk licenses tracks its public/private land ratio — GMU 15 is 77.5% public, so only ~22.5% of tags flow to the private pool (a thin pool; the units that mint tags are private-heavy like 13 and 34).
- **Scale:** ~14,600 authorizations went to landowners statewide in a recent year (vs. ~23,000 in the public draw); unit-wide Gila authorizations commonly trade in the low thousands to ~$12,500 (source: Luna Landing Retreat — Master Reference, E-PLUS Land Strategy — Luna).

## Why it matters

The tag is the **customer-acquisition mechanism**, not the product — the money is in bundling it into a guided hunt (lodging, guiding, processing) at $8,000–$15,000+, or brokering *other landowners'* tags. See [Trophy Hunting Economics](/notes/trophy-hunting-economics) and E-PLUS Land Strategy — Luna.
