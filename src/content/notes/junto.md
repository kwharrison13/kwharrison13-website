# Junto

[Benjamin Franklin](/notes/benjamin-franklin)'s 1730s mutual-improvement club — Kyle's archetype for the "association of the like-minded" that underpins a talent vortex. Per [Walter Isaacson](/notes/walter-isaacson)'s biography (as quoted in The Talent Vortex - Research), members "discussed issues of the day, debated philosophical topics, devised schemes for self-improvement, and formed a network for the furtherance of their own careers." Franklin, still a twenty-something printer, used the Junto to launch a run of community institutions — a lending library, fire brigade, night-watchmen corps, later a hospital, militia, and college — on the logic that "the good men may do separately is small compared to what they may do collectively." It's also the social extension of Franklin's self-improvement method (see [Self Improvement](/notes/self-improvement)).

Kyle ran his own Junto starting in 2019 — it met 35 times and produced 8 articles before failing — which he writes up in [The Importance of Arguing With Yourself](/essays/the-importance-of-arguing-with-yourself) and the draft What I Learned Running a Junto and Why It Failed.
