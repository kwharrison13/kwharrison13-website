# Southwestern Megadrought

**One-line:** *The emerging megadrought gripping southwestern North America since ~2000 — the driest 22-year period (2000–2021) in at least 1,200 years — driven partly by human-caused warming, and expressing not just as dry years but as permanent aridification.*

A durable climate-baseline concept surfaced in [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county). The single largest shift in [The Luna Landing](/notes/the-luna-landing) region's operating assumptions: the perennial mountain streams that made 1880s settlement possible are becoming less reliably perennial.

## How sources describe it

- **Driest in 1,200+ years.** Tree-ring soil-moisture reconstructions back to AD 800 show **2000–2021 was the driest 22-year period in at least 1,200 years**, exceeded only by a late-1500s megadrought (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- **Human fingerprint.** 2000–2021 precipitation ran ~8.3% below the 1950–1999 norm and temperatures ~0.91°C warmer; ~40% of the soil-moisture deficit is attributable to human-caused warming (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- **Aridification, not just drought.** A warmer atmosphere pulls more moisture from soil, snowpack, and streams even when precipitation is only modestly below average; warmer winters mean less snowpack and earlier melt, shortening the high country's slow-release season (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
- **Fire–water nexus.** The megadrought supercharges the fire regime (e.g. the 2012 Whitewater-Baldy Fire, the 2026 Sacaton Fire), and post-fire flash floods degrade watershed water supply for years — arguably the single largest emerging threat to regional water security (source: [Water in Catron County](/notes/water-in-catron-county)).
