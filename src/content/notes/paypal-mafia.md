# PayPal Mafia

The network of early [PayPal](/notes/paypal) employees — [Peter Thiel](/notes/peter-thiel), [Max Levchin](/notes/max-levchin), [Elon Musk](/notes/elon-musk), [Reid Hoffman](/notes/reid-hoffman), [Roelof Botha](/notes/roelof-botha), [Ken Howery](/notes/ken-howery), [Luke Nosek](/notes/luke-nosek), [David Sacks](/notes/david-sacks) and others — who went on to found or fund a large share of major tech companies. It is the canonical [tech mafia](/notes/tech-mafias) and central case study in [The Founders](/books/the-founders) ([Jimmy Soni](/notes/jimmy-soni)).

In Kyle's talent-vortex research it functions as the prototype of a [Talent Vortex](/notes/talent-vortex) whose "output" is a mafia. [Peter Thiel](/notes/peter-thiel) has said the reason they kept so many talented people at [PayPal](/notes/paypal) was that it was one of the few viable options after the dot-com bubble; [David Wallace](/notes/david-wallace) describes the mechanism as a "network hiring sort of effect" where "trust produced speed." The trio of [Peter Thiel](/notes/peter-thiel), [Elon Musk](/notes/elon-musk), and [Max Levchin](/notes/max-levchin) set a high bar — "not going to hire people that cannot operate at the level they can operate" — and [Founders Fund](/notes/founders-fund) is one of the downstream Thiel/Nosek/Howery vehicles the network spun out.
