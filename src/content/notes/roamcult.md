# RoamCult

**One-line definition:** *The grassroots believer-community around [Roam Research](/notes/roam-research) (the `#RoamCult` hashtag); Kyle's shorthand-tag for fandom-as-distribution, "cults," and storytelling communities.*

`#RoamCult` was the Twitter hashtag around which Roam's earliest fans organized — the same "extraordinary product love and grassroots believer community" Kyle flags in his Roam diligence. In [Books 2.0](/essays/books-20) Kyle credits Twitter and "the RoamCult" as where he met other tools-for-thought thinkers after discovering Roam during COVID lockdown. The tag sits adjacent to [Roam Brainstorm](/notes/roam-brainstorm) in his tools-for-thought vocabulary.

The community also had a darker edge that Kyle's notes preserve: a "One True Church" / "Roamanati" strain of elitism — some members holding "there is no room for any other than Roam" — that drove churn and pushed users toward Obsidian. Kyle used `#RoamCult` self-consciously as a joke about cult-like fandom ("Join a cult #RoamCult").

## How it shows up

- In the Cultivating Cults - Research notes, `#RoamCult` tags the essay's core argument: startups building "soft power" alongside their product win distribution — quoting [Mario Gabriele](/notes/mario-gabriele) that companies developing soft power "will create a story that wins."
- Roam-tagged in the [Soft Power](/notes/soft-power) notes (creator companies importing soft power into funds).

## Where it shows up

- The Storytelling of Investing - Research — Roam-tagged to the creator-company / soft-power M&A angle.
- Cultivating Cults - Research — the essay whose thesis (storytelling, chief evangelists, world-building) `#RoamCult` anchors.
- [Books 2.0](/essays/books-20) — Kyle's Roam origin story and the RoamCult on Twitter.
