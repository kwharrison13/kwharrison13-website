# Cassandras

**One-line definition:** *Andy Grove's term for the people — usually in middle management, often sales — who see a coming [Strategic Inflection Point](/notes/strategic-inflection-point) before senior leadership does, because they spend their time "outdoors where the winds of the real world blow in their faces."* — [Only the Paranoid Survive](/books/only-the-paranoid-survive)

## How sources describe it

- "Cassandras are usually in middle management; often they work in the sales Organization. They usually know more about upcoming change than the senior management... their genes have not been selected to achieve perfection in the old way." ([Only the Paranoid Survive](/books/only-the-paranoid-survive))
- Grove treats listening to them as "an investment in learning what goes on at the distant periphery of your business" — snow melts first at the periphery, so news from there helps sort signal from noise.
- He calls email his main channel for hearing from Cassandras once Intel grew too large to "manage by walking around."
- The catch: helpful Cassandras notice real 10X forces early, but their signals mix with false alarms — everyone hawking a new gadget hypes it, so real change and noise arrive looking the same at first.

## Where it shows up

- [Only the Paranoid Survive](/books/only-the-paranoid-survive) — Grove's mechanism for getting news of change from the organizational periphery before it becomes obvious from the data.

## Related concepts

- [Strategic Inflection Point](/notes/strategic-inflection-point) — what Cassandras give early warning of.
- [Inertia of Success](/notes/inertia-of-success) — the opposing force, pulling leadership toward denial of what the Cassandras report.
