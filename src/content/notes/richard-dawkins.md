# Richard Dawkins

Richard Dawkins is the evolutionary biologist and author of [The Selfish Gene](/notes/the-selfish-gene) (1976), which proposes a gene-centered view of evolution and, in Kyle's anti-library, sits on Charlie Munger's recommended-reading list from [Poor Charlie's Almanack](/books/poor-charlie-s-almanack).

Dawkins is best known in Kyle's corpus as the man who **coined the word "meme."** In [The Meme Economy](/essays/the-meme-economy), Kyle quotes the etymology: Dawkins introduced the meme in *The Selfish Gene* as "a unit of cultural transmission, similar to how genes transmit biological traits" — an idea, behavior, or style that spreads from person to person, subject to a kind of cultural natural selection. That framing anchors Kyle's recurring interest in [Memetics](/notes/memetics) and the stories that move markets and people.

Elsewhere in the corpus Dawkins appears as an exemplar of prominent atheism: in [-You Sit on a Throne of Lies-](/essays/you-sit-on-a-throne-of-lies) Kyle groups him with [Stephen Fry](/notes/stephen-fry), [Ricky Gervais](/notes/ricky-gervais), and [Sam Harris](/notes/sam-harris) when describing skepticism toward God, and quotes his line that the universe has "no design, no purpose, no evil, no good, nothing but pitiless indifference."
