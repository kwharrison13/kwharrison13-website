# Editing

Discussed in [How To Take Smart Notes](/books/how-to-take-smart-notes) as "work that needs focus" — distinct from typo-fixing, it involves rephrasing sentences, deleting redundancies, and adding passages to fill holes left in the argument. The book's point is that editing a ready rough draft is a *well-defined, motivating* task precisely because the finish line is in sight ("Everybody is motivated when the finish line is within reach"), in contrast to the open-ended anxiety of blank-page [Writing](/notes/writing). Elsewhere the book lists editing as just one of the many distinct attentional modes writing demands — "reading, understanding, reflecting... structuring, organizing, editing, correcting and rewriting" — each requiring a different kind of focus.

## Where this appears

- [How To Take Smart Notes](/books/how-to-take-smart-notes) — editing a prepared draft as the well-defined, motivating endgame of the slip-box method.
