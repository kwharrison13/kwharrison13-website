# Cloud Seeding

**One-line:** *Weather modification that enhances precipitation from clouds that already exist (it cannot make rain from dry air) — a real, low-cost, operationally routine but modest-effect (~5–15%, often ~10%) water-management tool in the interior West.*

A durable concept for [The Luna Landing](/notes/the-luna-landing)'s water-augmentation strand. The NM-specific playbook (economics, regulatory path, contacts, proposal) lives in [Southwestern New Mexico Cloud Seeding Pilot](/notes/southwestern-new-mexico-cloud-seeding-pilot); this is the reusable definition.

## How sources describe it

- **Two methods:** **glaciogenic (cold-cloud)** seeding — the dominant Western winter method, releasing trace **silver iodide** into supercooled clouds to grow ice crystals into snow; and **hygroscopic (warm-cloud)** seeding — salts for warmer summer rain (source: [Southwestern New Mexico Cloud Seeding Pilot](/notes/southwestern-new-mexico-cloud-seeding-pilot)).
- **Hard constraints:** needs pre-existing seedable clouds; effect is modest (~5–15%) and hard to prove (no unseeded counterfactual of the same storm); **cannot fight active fires** — and the driest, most fire-dangerous conditions are exactly when seeding is impossible.
- **Right framing = water supply / drought / snowpack**, never fire suppression. Reduced fire spread is only an indirect, downstream co-benefit.
- **Among the lowest-cost water-supply tools:** ~$2–3/acre-foot (Utah/Idaho) vs. ~$20/AF for alternatives; whole-state programs run ~$2–9M/yr; a single-basin ground-generator pilot can run a few hundred K/yr.
- **Governance (NM):** the Weather Control Act treats seeded precipitation as part of the natural water supply (no new appropriation); administered by the Interstate Stream Commission Weather Control Committee with a public-comment period; sponsored via a local Soil & Water Conservation District. Tribal consultation and burn-scar/wilderness exclusions are real requirements.
