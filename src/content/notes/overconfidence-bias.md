# Overconfidence Bias

Named in *[How To Take Smart Notes](/books/how-to-take-smart-notes)* as one of the [Mental Models](/notes/mental-models) behind why planning fails: academics and students systematically underestimate how long writing will take, even under their own worst-case estimates. In the cited study, only 45% of students finished within the time they were "99% likely" to make under any condition they thought possible (Buehler, Griffin, and Ross 1995) — and reminding them of past misses changed nothing, because "experience doesn't seem to teach students anything." The book notes wryly that it "has nothing to do with being a student" but "with being human": even the researchers who study the Overconfidence Bias admit they fall for it too ([Daniel Kahneman](/notes/daniel-kahneman) 2013, 245ff). The lesson the book draws is to be sceptical of outcome-focused planning and to picture the actual work required instead.
