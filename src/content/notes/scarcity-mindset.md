# Scarcity Mindset

**One-line definition:** *[Ben Horowitz](/notes/ben-horowitz): with a scarcity mindset it's hard to be happy no matter how rich or good-looking you are, because there's always somebody richer or better looking.*

The frame recurs across Kyle's reading in two registers — personal and civic. The personal version is [Ben Horowitz](/notes/ben-horowitz)'s: comparison-driven unhappiness where "there's always somebody richer or better looking," a line Kyle captured while reading Frederik Gieschen's writing, sitting in the same cluster as [H. L. Mencken](/notes/h-l-mencken)'s definition of wealth as "any income that is at least one hundred dollars more a year than the income of one's wife's sister's husband."

The civic version is the abundance-over-scarcity posture. In [Our Towns](/books/our-towns), the "Women of the Commons" in Eastport, Maine looked at a struggling town and, drawing explicitly on [Stephen Covey](/notes/stephen-covey), chose "abundance" over "scarcity" — the mindset shift the Fallowses tie to their whole "a handful of forceful people make the difference" thesis (the [Small Town Headhunter](/notes/small-town-headhunter) pattern). [The War on Normal People](/books/the-war-on-normal-people) sharpens it into a policy claim: [Andrew Yang](/notes/andrew-yang) argues economic insecurity is a "bandwidth tax" that makes a precarious population "dumber, more impulsive," so "any democracy" should "keep its population free of a mindset of scarcity in order to make better decisions."
