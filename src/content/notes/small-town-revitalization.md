# Small-Town Revitalization

**One-line:** *The playbook for turning a dying small town into a thriving destination — studied through Leavenworth (manufactured identity), Fredericksburg (authentic heritage, monetized), and Marfa (single patron + anchor institution) — the revitalizing-an-existing-town counterpart to greenfield [City Building](/notes/city-building).*

A durable concept for [The Luna Landing](/notes/the-luna-landing) (and Kyle's broader [City Building](/notes/city-building) / [Our Towns](/books/our-towns) reading). The full comps + Luna application live in Luna Landing Retreat — Master Reference; this is the reusable pattern.

## The three models

- **Leavenworth, WA — manufactured identity.** A dying timber town that *invented* a Bavarian theme in 1962 via a civic self-help process, hired architects, changed building codes, and still enforces it via a Design Review Board. Visitation reached the millions; residents only ~doubled. **Success shows up in the visitor economy, not resident count — and durability comes from enforcement.**
- **Fredericksburg, TX — authentic heritage, monetized.** Real German-immigrant identity + two stacked engines (National Museum of the Pacific War + a wine cluster that grew from ~5 to 50+ wineries). **Stack a second independent engine so each visit cross-sells the other.**
- **Marfa, TX — single patron + anchor institution.** Donald Judd's Chinati Foundation (arrived 1971, opened 1986) — intended as a permanent art installation, *not* a tourist attraction; tourism followed the authenticity. **The anchor must be authentic and non-commercial in core purpose; ~15-yr timeline.**

## The distilled pattern (four non-negotiables)

1. A single concrete **anchor**, not a vague tourism push.
2. A defensible, **ownable identity** story.
3. **Hard enforcement** of that identity (code/covenant).
4. **Patient capital** and a decade-plus horizon.

Plus the five-stage sequence: honest asset inventory → the anchor (yrs 0–3) → proof-of-concept before town-wide commitment → codify & enforce identity → stack a second engine → **plan for the success problem** (build affordability/resident-retention rules in *before* land values climb — Fredericksburg and Marfa both hit this wall).

## Luna's fit

Closest to a **Fredericksburg-style authentic-heritage play with a Marfa-style single-anchor catalyst** — not a Leavenworth invention, because Luna's ancestral settlement history is genuine. Two edges over the comps: a *real* story, and starting at 150 people (design/affordability rules can be written before anyone fights them). Access profile resembles Marfa's (remote, destination not day-trip), with one advantage Marfa lacks — the Springerville fly-in.
