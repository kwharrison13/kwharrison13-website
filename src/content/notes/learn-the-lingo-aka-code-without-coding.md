# Learn The Lingo aka Code Without Coding

> 💡 **Ideation-stage idea** — one of Kyle's [Entity Ideas](/notes/entity-ideas) business concepts, captured from his Roam notebook. Not an operating company; preserved here so the idea (and its research) survives.

*aka the “Learning the Lingo” course — teaching business people to “code without coding.”*

## Idea capture (from Roam)

- **Tags:** #Coding #Entrepreneurship #Education
- This is a series of courses geared towards expedited experience. In the digital age, our goal is to get people to the experience of people in their 30s but for when they’re in their 20s. We’re not creating a jack of all trades, master of none. We’re producing people with specialties but who can effectively communicate with other specialists. Breaking down departmental walls.
- How To Code Without Coding
Create a tutorial course directed at business people who don’t have the time or interest to learn how to code in detail but want to understand the background. Some of the courses can include “How Does Google Work?” and “How Does Facebook Make Money?” Most of the time when business people pitch their website to their programmer, they know what look they’re going for and they usually reference a website like this or Asana or Twitter. When they do that, they usually sound like idiots. This course teaches them how to talk the talk with their programmer, to understand the language even if they don’t know the details.
- **Course Ideas**
  - How Does Google Work and How Does Facebook Make Money?
    - Include a non-code tutorial regarding the processes of some of the most famous websites that will be illustrative examples for the business people to understand
    - Google, Facebook, LinkedIn, Twitter, Instagram, Tumblr, etc.
  - How Do You Know What a Programmer Is Worth
    - Valuation metrics: Programmers overvalue themselves and business people don’t understand what they’re paying for
    - Again, use illustrative examples to help the business people understand what kind of work, time, effort, experience goes into building a website and why it shouldn’t always cost as much as the programmer bids, or why it should cost SIGNIFICANTLY more than you would think it should
- I asked the following question to my network of entrepreneurs on Facebook. Some of their responses were insightful, others were pointless.
- What kind of lingo do business people need to know. Ideas?? Like, you need to build your company's website but you don't want to build it yourself. What do you wish you knew about programming when you talk to your programmer? What do you wish you knew about law when you talk to lawyer? Finances when you talk to your CFO? What are some insights you wish you had?
