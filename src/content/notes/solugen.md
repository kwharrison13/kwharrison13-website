# Solugen

**One-line:** *A Houston-based deeptech company — "a child of both YCombinator and MIT" — that makes bio-based chemicals, built by chemical engineers who treat company-building as a series of deep, hard-to-reverse bets.*

## What we know

- Started as a bio-based chemicals startup in an industry (chemicals) with very few startups, most of which have failed; Solugen's founders interviewed failed Cleantech 1.0 founders and found the #1 cause of failure was over-focusing on the technology instead of the customer problem (source: [Witnessing the Beginning of A Chemical Empire](/notes/witnessing-the-beginning-of-a-chemical-empire)).
- Built a chemical plant (a "Bioforge") in 2 years — work the founders originally targeted for 6 months and that most companies would take 10 years to complete, a ~5x speed-up credited partly to Elon Musk/Steve Jobs-style "reality distortion field" goal-setting.
- Deliberately based in Houston for **[Company-City Fit](/notes/company-city-fit)**: high density of chemical/biochemical engineers, a Houston/Austin cleantech boom, abundant clean energy and water supply, favorable chemical-plant zoning, and strong shipping/delivery routes.
- Marketing strategy leans on founder storytelling, word of mouth, podcasts, and appearances at industry venues (SynBioBeta, SXSW) rather than mass advertising — aimed at (1) attracting talent the company otherwise "doesn't deserve" and (2) being the first company customers think of in its niche.
- Product philosophy: give R&D tight, early boundaries on what the "minimum viable dataset" and customer requirements actually are, rather than let the technology wander; identifying a customer's "bleeding need" (e.g. an about-to-be-banned chemical, or a supplier going under) drives the sales motion.
- Has never had a high-pressure-valve-type safety incident, which the author attributes directly to Solugen's explicit emphasis on "doing no harm."
- One of Solugen's investors, Ian Rountree, and its CTO were the subject of a podcast that first drew the essay's author (an intern) to the company.
