# The Chosen (show)

*The Chosen* recurs in Kyle's gospel study as a vivid mental reference point — the show gives him a concrete, embodied image of [Jesus Christ](/notes/jesus) and the disciples that sharpens his scripture reading. In his Alma 13–16 study he notes that having "a really powerful visual in my mind of who [Jesus Christ](/notes/jesus) is has made this more poignant" — when he is being unkind, he pictures a face and a reaction that lands on him. In the Alma 36–38 study he ties the show to the doctrine of being called and chosen: "In *The Chosen* you see that with the selection of Christ's disciples," reading it alongside [Lectures on Faith](/notes/lectures-on-faith).

In Fulfilling the Measure of Our Creation - September 5, 2021, Kyle reaches for a specific scene — Mary Magdalene in *Episode 6: "Unlawful"* falling back into old habits and returning to Christ weeping, "I just can't live up to it" — as an image of the struggle to live up to discipleship and the mercy that meets it.

**Context:** *The Chosen* is a multi-season crowdfunded television drama created by Dallas Jenkins that depicts the life and ministry of Jesus Christ through the eyes of those who met him.

## Episode study notes

Kyle's episode-by-episode notes on *The Chosen* — synopsis, gospel themes, and characters — pulled from his Roam study pages.

### Season 1, Episode 1: "I Have Called You By Name"

**Synopsis:** In 2 A.D. Magdala, a father comforts his frightened young daughter by having her recite Adonai's words through Isaiah: "Fear not, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name. You are mine." Twenty-eight years later in Capernaum, the grown woman — Mary Magdalene — wakes disheveled and afflicted, accused in the market of being demon-possessed. [Nicodemus](/notes/nicodemus), a Pharisee from Jerusalem, is summoned to perform an exorcism and fails, later telling his wife Zohara, "only God Himself could have drawn them out." Matthew the Apostle, a tax collector, is treated as a social outcast by fellow Jews. [Simon Peter](/notes/peter) and Andrew the Apostle, drowning in tax debt, consider betraying fellow fishermen to Rome. At the low point of despair — contemplating suicide over a cliff — Mary is found by a man who calls her by name, "Mary of Magdala," and recites the same words of Isaiah, embracing her as she breaks down.

**Themes:**
- **Mental Illness** — Mary's fits read as Schizophrenia / Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder and as Exorcism; she takes comfort in a scripture her father shared (Isaiah 43:1; cf. Luke 8:2). Her attacks are tied to a memory of abuse by a Roman soldier. Matthew is portrayed with traits of Autism / Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD) — using cloth to open doors, throwing away shoes after mud, struggling with eye contact, "good with numbers."
- **Taxation** — Nicodemus is pressed to stop Jews fishing on the Sabbath (untaxed); Simon and Andrew bet on street fights and make a deal to inform on other Jews to clear their tax debt.
- **[Marriage](/notes/marriage)** — Simon and [Eden](/notes/garden-of-eden) ("fire and water"); Nicodemus and Zohara. "I wonder if what we can understand of the law is just as blurred. What if it's more beautiful and strange than we can ever imagine?"
- **Exorcism** — "Only God himself could have drawn them out." — [Nicodemus](/notes/nicodemus)
- **Obedience and forgiveness** — "God is with us when we live his **law to the letter**." — [Nicodemus](/notes/nicodemus)
- **Suicide** — Mary, "in hell," stops from jumping when drawn away by a bird.

**Characters:** Mary Magdalene, [Nicodemus](/notes/nicodemus), Zohara (fictional), Quintus, Matthew the Apostle, [Eden](/notes/garden-of-eden) (fictional), [Simon Peter](/notes/peter), Andrew the Apostle, [Jesus Christ](/notes/jesus).

### Season 1, Episode 2: "Shabbat"

**Synopsis:** Framed by a flashback to a Jewish mother teaching her son the meaning of Shabbat. Mary Magdalene, now well, is spotted by a member of the Jewish leadership who had witnessed her failed exorcism, and word spreads. Matthew evaluates Simon's tax claim before Quintus, who is impressed by Matthew's intelligence. Simon and Andrew drink with fellow fishermen (John the Apostle, James the Apostle, and their father Zebedee) while weighing betrayal. Nicodemus commits to a formal investigation of Mary's healing. Matthew reveals his father has disowned him: "he says he has no son." Mary nervously hosts her first Shabbat dinner — and [Jesus Christ](/notes/jesus) arrives unexpectedly.

**Themes:**
- **Police brutality** — Gaius batters a man in the street: "There is only one language that these people respond to. Learn to speak it."
- **Doubt** — Nicodemus questions Mary only to understand what he could not do.
- **[Parenting](/notes/parenting)** — Matthew's father disowned him; "people are confusing."
- **[Marriage](/notes/marriage)** — Simon and Eden argue over his hidden worries: "You answer to God. But when you come back you answer to both of us."

**Characters:** Mary Magdalene, Matthew the Apostle, Gaius, [Simon Peter](/notes/peter), Andrew the Apostle, John the Apostle, James the Apostle, [Nicodemus](/notes/nicodemus), [Jesus Christ](/notes/jesus).

### Season 1, Episode 3: "Jesus Loves The Little Children"

**Synopsis:** [Jesus Christ](/notes/jesus) camps in the woods outside Capernaum, communing with Heavenly Father. A little girl, Abigail (child), stumbles onto his camp and returns with her friend Joshua (child); Jesus welcomes the children into his work and teaches them. When his wooden lock and key finally work, he says, "it is good."

**Themes:**
- **[Revelation](/notes/revelation)** — Jesus withdraws to commune with God; washes his feet, then prays.
- **Wealth** — "Sometimes that's better" (of not being wealthy). He leaves a note: "I did not come only for the wealthy."
- **[Parenting](/notes/parenting)** — "I suppose. But I have some work to do. You might have to help." He doesn't require perfection, only inclusion. "Everyone has a much larger job than just their trade." *(Kyle: reminded me of Bishop Daniels's "you come too" — see Mission Counsel Journal.)*
- **Eye for an eye** — "The Lord loves justice. But maybe it is not ours to handle." (Deuteronomy 32:35; Mormon 8:20)
- **[Wisdom](/notes/wisdom)** — "Many times smart men lack wisdom."
- **The role of the Messiah** — Luke 4:18.
- **Be like little children** — "I hope my next students ask the same questions that you have."

**Characters:** [Jesus Christ](/notes/jesus), Abigail (child), Joshua (child).

### Season 1, Episode 6: "Indescribable Compassion"

The title episode of Christ's compassion: [Jesus Christ](/notes/jesus) cleanses a man with leprosy — an outcast who kneels and says, "Lord, if you are willing, you can make me clean," to which Jesus, moved with compassion, reaches out and touches him: "I am willing. Be clean." Jesus sends him to show himself to the priest. The episode dramatizes the Gospel accounts of the healing of the leper (Matthew 8:1–4; Mark 1:40–45; Luke 5:12–16) and sets Christ's willingness to touch the untouchable against the crowds and the watching religious leaders.

**Themes:** the reach of divine compassion to the outcast; the tension between the Mosaic law of cleanliness and Christ's healing touch; faith expressed as "if you are willing."

*(Kyle flagged this episode in his Roam study index; the summary above is supplied context — his own study notes are still to come.)*

### Season 2, Episode 6: "Unlawful"

Mary falls back into old habits and returns to Christ weeping, "I just can't live up to it." Christ responds: "Well that's true. But you don't have to. I just want your heart. The Father just wants your heart. Give us that. Which you already have. The rest will come in time." *(This scene is the one Kyle reaches for in Fulfilling the Measure of Our Creation - September 5, 2021.)*
