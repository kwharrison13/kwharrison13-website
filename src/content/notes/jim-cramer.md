# Jim Cramer

Financial-media personality who recurs across Kyle's corpus as a stand-in for the polarizing public-market pundit. In [HealthOS](/notes/healthos) he is quoted framing the investment case for a stock during January 2019 when [Apple](/notes/apple) was in free fall — "You know I always say, 'Own it, don't trade it.'"

Elsewhere he appears as the archetype of a personality who is "so wrong, so often" that it has become a running meme to do the opposite of whatever he says (the "inverse Cramer" account) — cited in [Intellectual Seat Belts](/essays/intellectual-seat-belts) and [Historical Futurism (Essay)](/essays/historical-futurism) on the risks of putting predictions out publicly. In [The Gospel of ARK Invest](/essays/the-gospel-of-ark-invest) he is grouped with other marketing-rich personas ([Joe Rogan](/notes/joe-rogan) among them) that people "either love or hate," with very little in-between.

## Where this appears

- [Intellectual Seat Belts](/essays/intellectual-seat-belts) — the "inverse Cramer" meme as a foil to "being wrong becomes your identity."
- [Historical Futurism (Essay)](/essays/historical-futurism) — cited on the criticism aimed at people who make public predictions.
- [The Gospel of ARK Invest](/essays/the-gospel-of-ark-invest) — an example of a polarizing, marketing-rich public persona.
